Binary papyrus segmentation can connect neighboring sheets and create tunnels,
making virtual unwrapping difficult even when voxel overlap is high.
We introduce \pLatS{}: learn a latent representation of individual sheets,
then predict it from CT and positive points with a transformer and MSE loss.
The same codes support automatic seed clustering and instance reconstruction.
On all 106 released Kaggle test cubes, 87.3\% of 764 eight-point predictions
have one component and no tunnels or cavities before cleanup.
We compare automatic segmentation with the Kaggle winner, distinguish model
defects from cuts caused by ignored labels, and examine downstream geometry
and ink on independently flattened, centimeter-scale surfaces.
The learned representation favors simple sheet topology without guaranteeing it.
