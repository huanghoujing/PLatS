Separating tightly packed papyrus sheets requires preserving their geometry
and identity: a small false-positive bridge can join neighboring layers even
when voxel overlap is high. We present \pLatS{}, which learns a single-sheet
latent space and predicts its codes from CT and positive point prompts.
A transformer trained with latent MSE uses the same representation for sheet
reconstruction and automatic seed clustering, postponing dense decoding until
instances are selected. Across 106 released Kaggle test cubes, 87.3\% of 764
eight-point sheet predictions have one component and no tunnels or cavities
before cleanup; single-point codes distinguish annotated sheet identities
with 0.912 AUROC. Automatic union scores remain below the Kaggle winner.
We analyze topology errors caused by ignore-mask erasure and demonstrate
independently flattened, centimeter-scale CT and ink outputs. The results
support a useful sheet prior while exposing incomplete coverage, instance
errors and stitching drift.
