\section{Discussion and conclusion}
PLatS uses a shared sheet code for prompted reconstruction and automatic
instance discovery. Raw topology and latent separability support this design,
while union scores, strict instance matching and large-page drift expose its
current limits. Matched direct-decoder and loss ablations are still needed to
isolate the contribution of the bottleneck and identity supervision. Measured
GPU time and memory are needed to quantify the benefit of clustering before
decoding. Spatially disjoint scroll evaluation and broader delivered ink
coverage are needed to establish generalization and reading benefit.
